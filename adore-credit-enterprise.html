08 · Compliance flags

Issues on the site that matter for the pitch.

These are flags, not a legal opinion. Product pages often say that removal, score increases, and timelines are not guaranteed. Those disclaimers do not erase the homepage graphics or the “same-day removal” name.

1. Homepage score jumps in 21–45 days

Under “REAL PEOPLE. REAL RESULTS,” the homepage shows Equifax 595 → 722 (+127, 21 days); TransUnion 611 → 734 (+123, 45 days); Experian 603 → 728 (+125, 45 days). No “results vary” disclosure was seen on the homepage itself (disclaimers do appear on Resources and several product pages). Atypical numbered results plus short timelines, on a credit-repair homepage, is a CROA / FTC testimonials issue. “Results vary” alone would not fully cure an atypical claim. Homepage · Resources disclaimer

2. “Same-day hard inquiry removal” naming

The product name and homepage copy “REMOVE / Negative Accounts” oversell what the fine print promises: dispute initiation, not guaranteed deletion. Inquiry product · Homepage

3. Upfront and monthly fees via Add to Cart

The site sells a $599 one-time “full repair,” a $250 per-account product, a $9.99 per-inquiry product, and a $75/month membership for immediate purchase. CROA generally restricts charging for credit-repair services before the contracted work is fully performed; the Telemarketing Sales Rule is stricter for certain phone sales. Whether they actually collect before work is complete cannot be verified from the site alone; the checkout presentation is still a flag. Services

4. $300 vs $599 on Credit Restoration

The product body, OG description, and schema description still say $300 one-time, while the cart price and schema offer are $599.00. That inconsistency is sloppy at best and can look like deceptive pricing. Product page

5. Authorized-user tradelines

Offered as “Credit Profile Enhancements,” with caveats, and the Resources guide says “Talk with April before adding any tradeline.” Still a high-risk add-on, and off-strategy if Conquest AI is not recommending tradeline products. Product page

6. Outcome-flavored booking copy

The Wix consultation description: “Improve your credit score with our professional credit repair services… setting you on a path towards better financial health.” Softer than a numeric guarantee, still outcome-flavored. Consultation page

7. SmartCredit $1-trial referral

Disclosed in the privacy policy as a referral relationship. The Resources page tells clients to start the trial and cancel after download. Worth knowing; not, by itself, a CROA violation. Privacy policy · Resources

09 · Conquest AI fit & pitch

Medium fit. Pitch the hours gap and the personal audience — not a new website.

Fit rating · Medium

Why not Low

This is a confirmed, in-market credit-repair business in DFW, with a named owner, published prices, a live site, online checkout, and a stated intent to build systems that can “operate beyond just me.” The weekday hours gap is unusually clear. Packages run from $75/month to $599 / $899, where captured daytime leads could pay for a receptionist if volume is there. She also has a personal Facebook audience of 8.3K. Voyage Dallas · Facebook · Hours

Why not High

The consumer brand and domain are only about six weeks old; public review proof is essentially zero; lead volume is unknown; she already has a free Wix calendar, so she may feel booking is solved; the operation still appears owner-centric (April personally reviews files); budget may be tight. “Adore Credit Enterprise LLC” was not found as a separate Texas filing. Tradelines and site claims add compliance sensitivity, so this should be a careful conversation, not a hard close on guaranteed-results marketing.

Pitch angle

Lead with daytime and after-hours coverage, not a website rebuild — she just launched a Wix site. Weekdays the phone is only advertised 6:00–10:00 PM CT, so most business-day calls in Dallas–Fort Worth are outside posted hours. Grace can answer those missed calls, text back immediately, and book the 30-minute consultation she already offers on Wix, then help work the free Discovery Assessment and any abandoned checkout. Hours · Booking

A second, Facebook-specific angle: her audience follows her personally (8.3K) rather than a business Page. The profile points people to Payhip and linktr.ee/AprilAdores, not to adorecreditenterprise.com or the 945 number, so DMs and link-in-bio traffic can arrive around the clock. An AI receptionist and speed-to-lead flow that catches those DMs-to-calls and inbound clicks fits that pattern. A Facebook business Page with reviews is also a gap. Facebook profile

She already said she wants systems so the company can grow beyond her. Follow-up, reactivation, and review-request automations fill that goal without adding daytime staff. Keep the pitch off “we’ll help you promise faster score jumps.” If anything, note that cleaning up homepage testimonials and the $300 vs $599 mismatch would make the brand easier to stand behind.
